- **Page numbering.** The PDF page index and the printed page number are identical
  throughout (verified on pp. 4, 17, 18, 21, 25). Every `p. N` in this file therefore
  refers to both.
- **Text.** Extracted with `pdftotext` (raw and `-layout`), then read page by page.
- **Tables and one figure encoded as images.** Five elements of the PDF carry no text layer
  and were extracted as images and read visually: **Table 1** (p. 4), **Table 7** (p. 25),
  **Figure 1** (p. 26), **Table 10** (p. 32), **Table 11** (p. 33). They are invisible to

  extraction interleaves their columns. **Table 4** (pp. 17–18), the table relevant to

- **No web search, no external source, no completion from general knowledge** was used at any

## Terminology defined by the source

### IC2025-TERM-01 — Grading, ranking, selection
- **type:** definition
- **source:** pp. 2–3 (Terminology)
- **statement:**
  > "Embryo grading is the evaluation of embryos using a specific set of criteria to assign a
  > quality score: the number, size, and shape of blastomeres, the degree of fragmentation, the
  > inner cell mass (ICM) and trophectoderm (TE) morphology and expansion, etc. Embryo ranking
  > refers to the procedure of prioritizing clinically usable embryos based on grading and other
  > assessment criteria, from most to least favourable for transfer. Embryos are ranked
  > according to their estimated potential for implantation and development, which is determined
  > by morphological and, when available, genetic factors. This is a prioritization of which
  > embryo(s) to transfer first. Embryo selection for transfer involves consideration of ranking
  > and other factors to select embryos for transfer into the uterus."

### IC2025-TERM-02 — Clinical use
- **type:** definition
- **source:** p. 2 (Terminology)
- **statement:**
  > "Clinical use of oocytes and embryos is defined as use for an IVF/ICSI treatment, biopsy/PGT,
  > cryopreservation, transfer, and donation."

### IC2025-TERM-03 — Morphokinetics
- **type:** definition
- **source:** p. 2 (Introduction)
- **statement:**
  > "As the term implies, morphokinetics represents the integration of morphology (the form and
  > structure of embryos) with kinetics (the dynamics of their development), providing a
  > comprehensive framework for understanding and evaluating embryo development in vitro."

### IC2025-TERM-04 — Morula (TLT sense)
- **type:** definition
- **source:** p. 21 (Section 5)
- **statement:**
  > "When using TLT, the term morula refers to the 'end of the compaction process' (Ciray et al.,
  > 2014)."

### IC2025-TERM-05 — Compaction span
- **type:** definition
- **source:** p. 20 (Initiation of compaction)
- **statement:**
  > "Compaction usually starts at the 8- to 16-cell stage. To be more precise, compaction spans
  > the phase between the point in time when any two blastomeres of the multicellular embryo
  > start to compact and the moment prior to the onset of blastocoel formation (Ciray et al.,
  > 2014)."

### IC2025-TERM-06 — Fragment
- **type:** definition
- **source:** p. 19 (Fragmentation)
- **statement:**
  > "A fragment can be defined as a membrane-bound extracellular cytoplasmic mass, often not
  > including chromosomes."

### IC2025-TERM-07 — Unequal cell size
- **type:** definition
- **source:** p. 20 (Uneven cleavage and cell size)
- **statement:**
  > "Unequal cell size has been defined as a 25% difference between the average diameter of the
  > smallest cells compared to the average of the largest cells (Meseguer et al., 2011; Ziebe,
  > 2013)."

### IC2025-TERM-08 — External nomenclature reference
- **type:** methodological statement
- **source:** p. 4 (Table 1 footnote); also cited at pp. 3, 20, 21
- **statement:**
  > "Nomenclature and definitions are based on Ciray et al. (2014)."
- **note:** Ciray et al. (2014), *Proposed guidelines on the nomenclature and annotation of
  dynamic human embryo monitoring by a time-lapse user group*, Hum Reprod 29:2650–2660, is the
  document to which this Consensus defers its morphokinetic nomenclature (the `tX` variable
  names and the definitions of dynamic events). **It is a separate publication and is not part
  of this corpus.** Any definition that this Consensus does not itself print must therefore be
  treated as *not available from this source*.

### IC2025-TIME-01 — Insemination method and early timing
- **type:** review finding
- **source:** pp. 3 and 5 (Section 1 narrative)
- **statement:** The Istanbul Consensus (2011) "differentiated between IVF- and ICSI-derived
  embryos only for one stage of development: early cleavage. Specifically, the 2-cell stage was
  proposed to be checked 2 h earlier post ICSI (26 ± 1 h postinsemination (hpi)), than IVF
  (28 ± 1 hpi)". Subsequent reports: "several studies reported that only the timing of the first
  cleavage was affected by fertilization method, with IVF embryos reaching the 2-cell stage
  significantly later than their ICSI counterparts (Dal Canto et al., 2012b; Kirkegaard et al.,
  2016)"; "comparative delays in IVF embryo development beyond the 2-cell stage of 1.5 ± 1.1 h
  (Bodri et al., 2015)"; a randomized controlled study of 373 sibling IVF and ICSI embryos
  "reported that only time to 2-cell (t2) was significantly delayed in IVF embryos (De Munck
  et al., 2022)"; a large TLT study of 2376 embryos "reported that t2 was 0.98 h earlier in
  ICSI-derived embryos (excluding those from donor sperm), while time to initiation of
  blastulation (tSB) and time to full blastocyst (tB) were 1.157 and 1.510 h later,
  respectively, compared with IVF-derived embryos (Barrie et al., 2021a)."
- **evidence_grade:** not graded in the narrative (no GRADE level is printed for Section 1 text).

### IC2025-TIME-02 — Other factors influencing developmental timing
- **type:** review finding
- **source:** p. 5 (Section 1 narrative)
- **statement:** Intrinsic and extrinsic factors investigated include BMI, age, culture media and
  oxygen concentration. On the two most studied patient variables: "age of gamete providers and
  BMI albeit with varying findings and **no meta-analyses or definitive studies yet available**".
  Ovarian stimulation protocol: "some apparent differences during early cleavage stages, but no
  effect on overall embryo quality". Temperature and pH: "lower temperature and culture medium pH
  drift (typically in an alkaline direction) are associated with slower embryo development".
  Oxygen: "a prospective study compared the developmental timings of embryos according to oxygen
  tensions, reporting significantly slower development in embryos cultured in 20% oxygen compared
  with 5% (Kirkegaard et al., 2013)."

### IC2025-TIME-03 — Chromosomal status and morphokinetic delay
- **type:** review finding
- **source:** p. 5 (Section 1 narrative)
- **statement:**
  > "A recent systematic review and meta-analysis incorporating over 40 000 embryos concluded
  > that ten morphokinetic variables were significantly delayed in aneuploid embryos, most
  > notably from t8 (development at the 8-cell stage) to the expanded blastocyst stage (Bamford
  > et al., 2022). Irregularities of cleavage, such as prolonged or rapid cell cycles, may be
  > associated with DNA repair activity, cellular rearrangement or failure to undergo cell cycle
  > checkpoints (Regin et al., 2022)."
- **qualification:** stated as an **association** ("were significantly delayed in", "may be
  associated with"). The source does not state a causal or diagnostic relation here.

### IC2025-TIME-04 — Embryonic sex and morphokinetics
- **type:** review finding
- **source:** p. 5 (Section 1 narrative)
- **statement:** Studies comparing developmental timings by chromosomal sex gave "conflicting
  results"; "More comparative large studies are needed, however, Fraire-Zamora et al. (2023)
  aimed to avoid confounding factors by using strict inclusion criteria and reported no
  significant differences in morphokinetics between male and female embryos."

### IC2025-TIME-05 — Clinical benefit of TLT algorithmic selection not demonstrated
- **type:** review finding / limitation
- **source:** p. 5 (Section 1 narrative)
- **statement:**
  > "However, two recent randomized controlled trials (RCTs) found no improvement in ongoing
  > pregnancy rate or cumulative live birth rate or live birth rate per transfer, when using TLT
  > algorithmic selection (Ahlström et al., 2022; Kieslinger et al., 2023), corroborating the
  > findings of the latest Cochrane review (Armstrong et al., 2019)."
  >
  > "Although the studies are heterogeneous and drawing strong conclusions is difficult, TLT
  > studies can help inform and optimize static assessment timing windows in the IVF laboratory."

### IC2025-TIME-06 — Heterogeneity of the underlying literature
- **type:** limitation
- **source:** p. 2 (Introduction)
- **statement:**
  > "Hundreds of papers have been published on embryo assessment. The studies are mostly
  > retrospective and heterogeneous with respect to some key parameters including patient
  > population, outcome measures, control for confounders, laboratory procedures, and embryo
  > culture conditions. Furthermore, morphokinetic studies, as well as classical morphological
  > studies, may be influenced by maternal age, smoking status, ovarian stimulation protocols,
  > and insemination methods, among other factors."

### IC2025-TIME-07 — Inter-observer subjectivity of morphology assessment
- **type:** limitation
- **source:** p. 2 (Introduction)
- **statement:**
  > "Yet, morphology assessment remains largely subjective and prone to inter- and
  > intra-observer and inter-laboratory variability (Arce et al., 2006; Baxter Bendus et al.,
  > 2006; Martínez-Granados et al., 2017; Storr et al., 2017)."

### IC2025-TIME-08 — Days are a simplistic description since TLT
- **type:** review finding
- **source:** p. 5 (Section 1 narrative)
- **statement:**
  > "Since the publication of the original Istanbul Consensus (2011), the convention of
  > describing the timing of preimplantation development in terms of number of days (post
  > insemination) has come to be viewed as simplistic, largely due to the facility to observe
  > the developing embryo almost continuously, in minutes and hours, rather than days, using TLT
  > imaging."

### IC2025-TIME-09 — General description of cleavage
- **type:** review finding
- **source:** p. 3 (Section 1, opening)
- **statement:**
  > "Development of the human embryo begins with fertilization and continues with a series of
  > restrictive mitotic events (cleavage) each of which doubles the cell number as the embryo
  > develops from a single cell into a multicellular blastocyst (Ciray et al., 2014). At
  > fertilization, once the two pronuclei break down, paternal and maternal chromosomes are
  > assembled into a bipolar mitotic spindle, before sister chromatids are orderly segregated in
  > the first two blastomeres at first cleavage. The resulting undifferentiated daughter cells
  > are expected to be genetically identical."
- **qualification:** this is a narrative description of the normal course of cleavage. It is
  **not** an ordering rule, and the source does not use it to define which intermediate cell
  counts (3-, 5-, 6-, 7-cell) are expected or unexpected. See IC2025-CP4-06 and Table 5 for what
  the source does state about expected cell numbers.

### IC2025-TIME-10 — First mitotic division is error-prone
- **type:** review finding
- **source:** p. 3 (Section 1)
- **statement:**
  > "However, recent studies have investigated the fine details of the first event of chromosome
  > segregation in the human embryo, revealing a highly error-prone mechanism (Currie et al.,
  > 2022). Although the exact timing is yet to be elucidated, embryonic genome activation is well
  > underway by the 8-cell stage, with the concomitant degradation of maternal transcripts."

### IC2025-TIME-11 — Assessment must follow predefined times
- **type:** review finding
- **source:** p. 3 (Section 1)
- **statement:**
  > "Since the competence of the human embryo is also reflected in its developmental timeline,
  > assessment of morphology should be in accordance with predefined times."

### E. Limitations of the evidence level
- **IC2025-AC-E01** — every outcome association reported for the grouped abnormal-cleavage
  pattern is graded **Low (⊕⊕○○)** and rests on 3–4 observational studies; *embryo quality* and
  *live birth rate* are **N/R** in Table 4.
- **IC2025-AC-E02** — the three patterns are **never reported separately** in this source: the
  evidence row groups them, and the recommendations name them together. No per-pattern effect
  size, prevalence or outcome is given.
- **IC2025-AC-E03** — detection requires **TLT**; the source states these events "would in most
  cases be missed with static observations" (p. 21) and that trichotomous cleavage "is only
  detectable by TLT" (p. 19).
- **IC2025-AC-E04** — the general limitation on TLT algorithmic selection applies (IC2025-TIME-05,
  IC2025-CP4-09): limited good quality evidence of better clinical outcomes following TLT embryo
  selection.
- **IC2025-AC-E05** — the source's own nuance on cumulation (IC2025-AC-C06): a **single** early
  abnormality among those that reach blastocyst stage was **not** associated with aneuploidy;
  two or more were.

### Explicitly forbidden equivalences (scope guard for any consumer of this corpus)
These statements are **not** supported by this source and must never be derived from it:
- a **phase skip** in an annotated or predicted developmental-stage sequence **is not** a direct
  cleavage;
- a **phase regression / return** in a predicted sequence **is not** a reverse cleavage;
- an **irregular sequence of phase labels is not** an irregular chaotic division.
This source defines none of the three patterns (IC2025-AC-B01) and characterises all three as
cell-division events observed by time-lapse microscopy, not as properties of a stage-label
sequence.

### IC2025-T9 — Table 9 (consensus scoring system for blastocysts)
- **type:** table (definition / grading scale)
- **source:** p. 31, **Table 9**
- **printed title:** "Consensus scoring system for blastocysts."

**Stage of expansion**

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Early blastocyst: blastocoel less than half of the volume of the embryo. |
| 2 | Blastocyst: blastocoel that is half of or greater than half of the volume of the embryo. |
| 3 | Full blastocyst: blastocoel completely fills the embryo. |
| 4 | Expanded blastocyst: blastocoel larger than that of the early embryo, with a clearly thinning zona. |
| 5 | Hatching blastocyst: trophectoderm starting to herniate though the zona. |
| 6 | Hatched blastocyst: blastocyst has completely escaped from the zona |

**Grade — ICM**

| Grade | Description |
|---|---|
| A | Prominent, easily discernible, with many cells that are compacted and tightly adhered together. |
| B | Easily discernible, with several cells that are loosely grouped together. |
| C | Very few cells visible. |
| D | No visible cells, or presence of degenerating cells. |

**Grade — TE**

| Grade | Description |
|---|---|
| A | Many cells forming a cohesive epithelium. |
| B | Moderate number of cells forming a loose epithelium. |
| C | Few and larger cells with poor epithelia formation. |
| D | Sparse or degenerating cells surrounding the ICM |
